# Lesson 00: What You'll Master + How to Use This Course
**Section 0: Orientation | Duration: ~5 min (theory only)**

---

## HOOK (0:00)

> You've done it. Two hundred LeetCode problems. Maybe three hundred.
>
> And you *still* freeze when the Google interviewer shares a problem you haven't seen.
>
> Here's the uncomfortable truth: **grinding problems doesn't work.** Not because you're not smart — because you're learning the wrong unit. You're memorizing *problems*. Google tests *patterns*.

---

## THE PROMISE (0:40)

> By the end of this course, you'll look at a brand-new problem and, within 60 seconds, think: *"Sorted array, find a pair — that's two pointers. I've got this."*
>
> That single skill — **recognizing the pattern fast** — is what separates the candidates who get the offer from the ones who did twice as many problems and still got rejected.
>
> We'll cover **15 patterns**. That's it. Fifteen. Every one of the ~50 most-asked Google problems maps to one of them. Learn the 15, and 300 problems collapse into "oh, I know which tool this is."

---

## HOW THE COURSE IS BUILT (1:40)

> **20 sections, 111 short lessons, ~16 hours.** Each pattern gets its own section: the idea, then 4–7 real Google problems solved with it, easy to hard.
>
> Every problem lesson has the same two halves:
>
> **Part 1 — The Pattern (~3 min):** the *signal* that tells you to use it, a real-world picture, and a quick recognition check.
>
> **Part 2 — The Solve (~6 min):** and this is the important part. Every problem is broken into **three layers**:
>
> 1. **Brute force** — the obvious solution, and *why it times out*.
> 2. **Optimised solution** — the pattern applied, faster.
> 3. **Space optimization** — cutting extra memory when we can.
>
> You'll never just see the final answer. You'll see how to *get* there — because that's what you have to do live, in the room.

**[SLIDE: Two halves — "The Pattern" (3 min) + "The Solve: Brute → Optimised → Space" (6 min)]**

---

## WHY THREE LAYERS? (2:50)

> Because that's exactly what the interviewer wants to watch.
>
> Jump straight to the optimal solution and they think: *"Did they memorize this?"* Start with brute force, say its Big-O, then improve it — and they think: *"This person can solve problems they've never seen."*
>
> The third layer, space, is the one most candidates skip. It's also the one that turns a "hire" into a "strong hire." When you finish coding and add *"and I can make this O(1) space by reversing in place instead of copying,"* you just separated yourself from 80% of the field.

---

## HOW TO USE IT (3:40)

> Three rules to get the most out of this:
>
> **1. Do the ACTIVE RECALL problem before the next lesson.** Every lesson ends with a near-twin problem. Attempt it *before* you move on. Watching me solve isn't learning — *you* solving is.
>
> **2. Say it out loud.** Every solution, narrate it like an interview. Brute force, complexity, optimise, space. The `resources/` folder has a 6-Step Framework card — print it.
>
> **3. Don't skip the behavioral section.** Half the Google loop is Leadership Principles. We build your 6 STAR stories at the end. Coding gets you *in*; behavioral gets you the *offer*.

---

## 3-POINT SUMMARY (4:40)

> 1. **Patterns beat problems.** 15 patterns cover the Google interview. Learn to *recognize*, don't memorize.
> 2. **Every solve is three layers:** brute force → optimised → space. That's the skill the interviewer scores.
> 3. **Do the recall problems, narrate out loud, and don't skip behavioral.**

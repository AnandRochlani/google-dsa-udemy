# 🧭 Googleyness, GCA & Leadership → Your Story Map

> Google doesn't grade behavioral on a list of principles. Every interviewer scores you on **four attributes**, and behavioral + "how you think out loud" feed directly into them. Know the four, and shape your stories — in **STAR** form (Situation, Task, Action, Result) — to hit them.

---

## The four attributes Google actually scores

| Attribute | What it is | Where it's scored |
|---|---|---|
| **GCA — General Cognitive Ability** | *How you approach a novel problem.* Structure, clarifying questions, breaking it down, trade-offs, learning from a hint. | **Every coding round** (this is why you narrate) + hypothetical questions |
| **RRK — Role-Related Knowledge** | Depth in the actual craft — DS&A, systems, your specialization. | Coding + design rounds |
| **Leadership** | Not title. *Emergent* leadership: you step up, mobilize others, and step back when someone else should lead. | Behavioral round |
| **Googleyness** | Comfort with ambiguity, bias to action, humility, collaboration, doing the right thing, intellectual curiosity. | Behavioral round + woven through every interaction |

> **The big shift from Amazon:** Amazon assigns each interviewer 2–3 Leadership Principles. Google assigns each interviewer some subset of these four **attributes** and asks for stories + a live problem. GCA is the one you're being scored on *while you code* — so the think-out-loud habit this course drills is literally half your rubric.

## What interviewers probe for (and a story seed for each)

| Signal they listen for | Seed a story from… |
|---|---|
| **Structured thinking under ambiguity** (GCA) | A vague, under-specified problem you scoped before building |
| **You learned something fast** (GCA / Googleyness) | Picked up a new tech/domain to unblock a project |
| **Emergent leadership** | You stepped up to drive a project nobody owned |
| **You brought others along** (Leadership) | Aligned people who initially disagreed |
| **Bias to action + comfort with ambiguity** (Googleyness) | Shipped a reversible decision fast, learned, iterated |
| **Humility / admitting wrong** (Googleyness) | Owned a mistake and fixed it openly |
| **Collaboration over ego** (Googleyness) | Gave up your design because a teammate's was better |
| **Deep craft** (RRK) | A hard bug you chased to the real root cause |
| **Doing the right thing** (Googleyness) | Raised a quality/ethics concern even when inconvenient |

## The STAR template (fill in, memorize the shape not the words)

- **S — Situation:** 1–2 sentences of context. *When and where.*
- **T — Task:** what *you specifically* had to do. Use "I," not "we."
- **A — Action:** the bulk. The concrete steps *you* took. This is what's scored.
- **R — Result:** the outcome, **quantified** if at all possible ("cut latency 40%," "saved 6 eng-hours/week"). Add what you learned.

> **Watch the "we" trap.** Interviewers stop you and ask "but what did *you* do?" Pre-decide your individual contribution in every story.

## Build 6 reusable stories (each flexes across attributes)

You don't need a story per signal. You need **6 strong ones**, each tagged to the 2–3 attributes it can answer:

1. **A shipped project you drove** → Leadership, GCA, Googleyness (bias to action)
2. **A hard bug / deep debug** → RRK, GCA (structured root-causing)
3. **A disagreement you handled well** → Leadership, Googleyness (collaboration, humility)
4. **A failure / mistake you owned** → Googleyness (humility), Leadership (accountability)
5. **A time you learned something fast** → GCA, Googleyness (curiosity)
6. **A time you scoped an ambiguous ask** → GCA (structure), Googleyness (comfort with ambiguity)

> Tag each of your stories with its attributes in `sessions/L109_STAR_Stories.md` as you write them.

## How coding + behavioral connect at Google

GCA is the through-line. The *way* you narrate the coding problem — clarify, state the brute force, name the trade-off, test — is the **same skill** the behavioral round probes with "tell me about a time you tackled something you'd never seen." Practice the coding narration until it's automatic, and half your behavioral score comes for free.

> **The one-line reminder:** at Google, *how you think* is on the rubric. Say the approach out loud, always.
